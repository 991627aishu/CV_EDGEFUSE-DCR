Theoretical Foundations & Problem Statement

Why Depth Completion Matters

Autonomous navigation, robotic grasping, and AR/VR depend on precise 3D geometric scene structure. However, raw physical depth sensors face fundamental optical and mechanical limitations.

01

Sensor Sparsity & Dropout Dilemma

Mechanical LiDAR (e.g. Velodyne HDL-64E) captures only 4–6% of scene pixels. Structured light (Microsoft Kinect) drops out completely on shiny, dark, or specular surfaces, leaving extensive voids.

• KITTI: ~95.6% void pixels
• Kinect: Specular dropouts
02

The Naive Interpolation Trap

Standard bilinear and bicubic interpolation treat depth as a continuous smooth field, averaging across foreground and background. This blurs object silhouettes and causes disastrous "flying pixels".

• High boundary error (F1: ~0.52)
• Foreground-background depth bleed
03

High-Resolution RGB Priors

Companion RGB cameras provide dense photometric details at full resolution. Sharp color and luminance transitions correlate strongly with real 3D physical boundaries and object contours.

• RGB Sobel/Canny prior
• Cross-modal edge guidance
04

Confidence-Guided Weighting

Not all sensor measurements are equally trustworthy. Distance-attenuated confidence weighting assigns high certainty to measured seeds while suppressing unreliable noise in unmeasured regions.

• Pixel-wise confidence matrix $C(u, v)$
• Dynamic spatial decay
05

Bilateral Discontinuity Preservation

Fast bilateral edge refinement enforces smoothness only where photometric color variance is minimal, halting depth propagation abruptly at true physical occlusion boundaries.

• Zero depth bleed across silhouettes
• Texture-copy suppression
06

The EdgeFuse-DCR Solution

By coupling a 1.31M parameter dual-branch CNN with a deterministic confidence-weighted bilateral solver, EdgeFuse-DCR delivers superior quantitative accuracy (lowest RMSE) without texture bleeding.

• State-of-the-Art RMSE across 3 benchmarks
• Fast CPU-friendly inference
User Workflow & Operations

How to Use EDGEFUSE-DCR

Follow this 8-step workflow to reproduce benchmarks, explore genuine sensor datasets, or execute depth reconstruction on custom imagery.

STEP 01

Select a Dataset

Choose between NYU Depth V2 (indoor structured light), KITTI (outdoor LiDAR), or Middlebury 2021 (stereo disparity).

STEP 02

Pick a Scene

Browse verified paired scenes using dual thumbnails. Click "Load in Workspace" to sync RGB, input depth, and Ground Truth.

STEP 03

Upload Custom Inputs

Upload custom RGB photos (.png/.jpg) and depth maps (.npy / 16-bit PNG). Ordinary color photos in the depth field are automatically rejected.

STEP 04

Configure Scale

Set calibration to Millimeters (16-bit PNG), KITTI format (1/256m), or Metric Float32 to ensure physically meaningful distances.

STEP 05

Reconstruction Setup

Choose upsampling factor (2×, 4×, 8×), select algorithm (A0 baselines through A5 EdgeFuse-DCR hybrid), and toggle texture suppression.

STEP 06

Execute Pipeline

Click "Execute Depth Reconstruction". PyTorch CPU inference runs asynchronously, updating KPI cards and visualizations.

STEP 07

Inspect Diagnostics

Switch tabs across Primary Reconstruction, Ablation Progression (A0 vs A2 vs A5), Edge Sobel Gradients, and Error Heatmaps.

STEP 08

Export Research Data

Download raw reconstructed .npy float32 arrays, 16-bit millimeter PNGs, confidence maps, error heatmaps, and JSON benchmark reports.
